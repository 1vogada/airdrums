# Version 2: the council's build spec

Written by the chair of a council of seven (drum teacher, rhythm-game designer, product and UX designer,
front-end architect, audio and sync engineer, the player, scope realist) after they pitched, voted on 121
features and argued. **Where this file and [decisions.md](decisions.md) disagree, decisions.md wins**:
it holds the changes made after two outside critics went through this spec.

## In short

Version 2 turns Air Drums from a menu of five note views into a song-first practice and play-along tool. The front door is a search box on Home (Chorus Encore is the only search source; charts are grouped into songs), with the user's own Liked and Recent lists, a Continue card and a hand-picked starter list under it. Each song has a page that names what will sound: Play (the site's own synth drums and click on the site's own clock), Learn, Play along with Spotify, or 'I'll start the music myself' (today's silent clock with line-up by ear). The five views become a switch on one rebuilt play screen whose set-once controls sit behind chips and a menu. Learn loops one part of the song on the site's clock with a counted bar before every pass, a Listen pass first, tempo from 50 to 100%, bass and snare first, automatic steps, a stored 'learned' tick per part and the learner's own 'Got it' when the site cannot hear. Runs end in a results view with a bar per part and a door back into Learn. Spotify is only made safer (fewer requests, expired login, quota pause, a zero-call hand-off); nothing new is asked of it. Everything stays in the browser, js/render.js and the microphone code are not edited, tests/ear_e2e.mjs passes unchanged, and every item is proven by a stubbed headless rig that is built first and run against today's UI.

### B03. Move the play pipeline into session.js with no UI change; one clock shape

Create js/session.js and move into it, with their logic unchanged, everything in js/main.js between the routing and the setup blocks: setSong, loadPicked, applyRough, showOffset's text, setFine, dropFine, nudge, tick, struck, the mic hooks, startListen, learn, byEar, playManual and demo. Clock: ManualClock is deleted from js/spotify.js and OwnClock (js/clock.js, same set / toggle / seekTo / position / playing / at / stop surface) takes its place at rate 1 with no loop. Every clock gets kind and can: OwnClock {kind:'own', can:{rate:true, loop:true, seek:true, sound:true}}, Follow {kind:'spotify', can:{rate:false, loop:false, seek:'premium', sound:false}}; every 'instanceof ManualClock' becomes a test of clock.kind or clock.can. The session has a source: 'spotify' (Follow) or 'self' (own clock, silent, the user starts their own music: lyric shift on the notes and line-up by ear ON, exactly today's typed-song and ?demo= behaviour). Follow gets start() and stop() that really end its polling loop; session.stop() (called whenever the play screen is left) stops the clock, cancels the frame and flushes sound. ### B05. Song lookup: search as you type, charts turned into songs

js/net.js (DOM-free, runs in Node): one queue for api.enchor.us: one request in flight, at least 700 ms between sends, every call abortable; a 5xx or network error is retried once after 1 s; a 429 or 503 means no request for 30 s (the rate-limit headers cannot be read from page script) while the list shows 'The chart site is busy. Trying again in 30 s' counting down, then one automatic retry. Range requests to files.enchor.us do not go through the queue. js/catalog.js (pure, DOM-free): slim(row) cuts a raw answer row at once to a record of about 400 bytes: md5, name, artist, album, year (first four digits), genre, charter, length (song_length, as fit() uses today), playLength (notesData.effectiveLength, for display), tier (diff_drums), levels {expert:n, hard:n, medium:n, easy:n} from noteCounts, nps (expert notes / playLength), drumType, lyrics, twoKick, delay, art (albumArtMd5), pack, modified; raw rows of 3 to 70 KB are never kept. songs(rows) de-duplicates by md5, drops a charter's older upload of the same title, hides whole-album charts (over 15 minutes or 'Full Album' in the name) and '(6 Fret)' uploads, labels versions Live, Acoustic, Demo, Remix, Cover and 2x pedal (from the name or has2xKick), and groups versions by songKey (normalised first artist | cleaned title, from js/library.js). Version order inside a song: unlabelled before labelled, then known real kit (a flag remembered per md5 after a load; charter Harmonix is assumed real until a load says otherwise), then lyrics, then cymbals told apart, then more levels, then newest. Song order: songs whose title or artist equals the query first, then the chart site's order of first appearance, songs whose best version is labelled last. retries(query) gives at most two fallback queries: (1) accents folded and a trailing apostrophe or punctuation on the last word removed (apostrophes inside a word stay), (2) the last word dropped. searchCharts() in js/charts.js keeps its name and return shape (tests import it) but goes through net.js and slim() with per_page 25 and drumsReviewed false. UI (js/screens/home.js): typing 2 or more characters searches 400 ms after the last key, Enter at once; the address becomes #/search/{query} with history.replaceState while typing; the previous request is aborted and a stale answer dropped by token. While waiting, matching songs from the user's own library show at once under 'Your songs' (also when the chart site is down). On zero results the retries run by themselves and the list says: Nothing for 'x'. Showing results for 'y'. If all fail: No drum part found for 'x'. Rock and metal are covered best. Try only the title or only the artist. 'Show more' asks page + 1 until an answer brings no new song or page x 25 >= found. Row (56 px; one component for search and library, in js/ui.js): a 48 px box with a letter tile and, over it, the picture (https://files.enchor.us/{art}.jpg, or the Spotify cover a library song has) set only once the row has been on screen for 300 ms (IntersectionObserver, decoding async; on error the tile stays); title; 'Artist - Year'; length; how busy as one word with four dots (tier 0-1 Gentle, 2-3 Moderate, 4-5 Busy, 6 and up Intense, nothing when unknown; notes per second in the tooltip); at most two badges 'Real kit' and 'Auto sync' (has lyrics) plus one caution label (Live, Cover, 2x pedal, Expert only); 'n versions'; the best grade letter when played; a heart; a play button. Charter names stay off the row. Keys: Up and Down move a highlight (the first row is highlighted by default), Enter opens the song page, Ctrl+Enter or the row's play button starts the default version on the site's drums at once, H likes the highlighted row, Esc clears the field. The last 50 answers stay in memory so Back is instant.

## Learning mode

### Flow

(1) Open a song, press Learn (or a row in its parts list, 'Learn {weakest part}' on the results, 'Learn this song' in the Spotify dock, or Continue on Home). (2) The site picks the part: the one asked for, else the one last practised, else the first part that is not learned. It picks the start: 70% tempo, and Bass + Snare when the part is busier than 5 notes a second, otherwise all drums; a part practised before resumes where it was left. (3) Ready: 'Sole Verse 1 - 16 s - 32 notes - 70% - Bass + Snare. Enter to start.' (4) The first pass of a part never tried is LISTEN: one counted bar, then the site's drums play the whole part while the notes scroll and the pads light; nothing is judged. L skips it or asks for it again. (5) YOUR TURN: the part loops by itself; every pass starts with a counted bar (1 2 3 4 in large numerals, from the chart's own beats) in which the last verdict and what happens next are shown: '82% - Close', 'Clean - + Hi-hat comes in', 'Clean - up to 80%', 'Again - down to 60%'. The verdict is the share of the chosen drums' notes that were hit inside 130 ms of real time; a wrong-drum guess from the mic counts as a hit and extra hits cost nothing. (6) With Auto on the site steps by itself: two clean passes (85% or more) in a row bring in the next drums at the same tempo, and once all drums are in, raise the tempo 10%; two passes in a row under 60% lower it 10%, never under 50%. Up, Down and K always override. (7) One clean pass at 100% with all drums marks the part learned: a tick on the strip, the song page and Home. With Move on ticked the loop moves to the next unlearned part, which opens with a Listen pass. (8) When the site cannot hear (no mic, no keys) it invents no percentage: 'Not scoring: no hits heard. Press G when it feels clean.' G = Got it marks the part learned on the learner's own say-so, shown as an outlined tick, with Undo. (9) When every part is learned: 'All parts learned. Play the whole song (Enter)': a normal run on the site's drums (or along with Spotify from the song page) that ends in results, whose part bars send the learner back to any part that slipped.

### Sound

All sound comes from the site's own synthesised kit (oscillators and noise, no samples, nothing downloaded), with levels fixed by numbers and asserted in the rig. Listen pass: the whole drum part of the looped part, the drums you are about to play at full level and the others at a third, plus the click. Counted bar: an accent click and clicks. Your turn depends on where the sound goes. Through speakers with the mic on (the default assumption): silence after the counted bar and a beat dot pulsing on the practice line, because the microphone cannot tell the site's drums or click from the kit's; the site never scores its own sound and never guesses by timing. With 'I'm on headphones' ticked, or with the keyboard only: the click as set, and the guide playing the drums you left out so the groove stays whole. In whole-song Play on the site's drums the guide plays everything; if the mic is on through speakers the run says 'Not scored by mic' until Guide is turned off or headphones are ticked. Guide and click each have their own switch and volume in the Sound chip. Spotify makes no sound in Learn and the site sends it nothing: one line asks the user to pause it.

### Clock

Learn always runs on the site's own clock (OwnClock): tempo 50 to 100% in steps of 10, a loop, a pass counter, and it may start below 0:00. Time is the chart's own time: no line-up, game offset 0, line-up by ear off. The loop is [the part's own marker minus one counted bar, the part's end plus 150 ms of real time], so pickups are kept, every pass has its count-in and the wrap falls where nothing is judged. The counted bar is the K beats before the marker at the tempo there (K = the beats of that bar; never an assumed four). The notes move slower with the tempo, and judging windows stay 45 / 85 / 130 ms of real time at every tempo. A change of tempo, drums or part takes effect within one frame, flushes the scheduled sound and restarts the pass at the counted bar. Sound is scheduled about 0.14 s ahead on the audio clock (plus the reported output latency) from both the frame and a 25 ms timer; late sounds are dropped, never bunched. Hiding the tab pauses. Spotify meanwhile: following stops on entering Learn and no request is sent; Spotify cannot be slowed or looped, so tempo, loop, guide and drums controls do not exist while following it, and one button there reopens the same song in Learn.

### Progress

Per song, version (md5) and part (id = the part's start in milliseconds): tries, the best clean tempo with its accuracy, the drums it was reached with, the last tempo and drums used, and whether it is learned and how (by a judged pass or by hand). Shown as marks on the part strip (empty, half, filled tick, outlined tick for by hand), 'n of m parts learned' and a state per row on the song page, 'n of m parts' on Liked and Recent rows, and the Continue card on Home, which reopens the exact part, tempo and drums. A practice pass is never a run and never a best. Full runs are kept separately (the last 12 per song) with tempo, drums, input and coverage; a best exists per version and level and only for a run at 100% with all drums that covered 90% of the notes. Another version of the song has its own parts and its own progress, and the song page says so.

### Tools

- Part picker: the part strip and Left / Right; parts come from the chart's own sections, cleaned, short ones merged, long ones cut into 8-bar pieces
- Loop of one part with a counted bar before every pass
- Tempo 50 to 100% in tens (Up / Down)
- Drums to play: Bass + Snare, + Hi-hat, All drums (K); works on every chart, including the 40% that have only an expert level
- Auto: tempo and drums step by themselves from the pass results (switch in the dock)
- Listen pass: automatic the first time a part is met, L on request
- Click on or off (C); guide and click volume and 'I'm on headphones' in the Sound chip
- Got it (G): the learner's own mark when the site cannot hear, with Undo
- Move on: after 'learned' go to the next unlearned part by itself
- Whole song slower: the tempo stepper also works in Play on the site's drums (such a run is never a best)
- Hit delay for the microphone on the site's clock: fitted from the learner's own hits, nudged with [ and ], reset in Setup
- Results after a full run: one bar per part and 'Learn {weakest part}'

## Data model

Everything about the user stays in localStorage under the prefix 'airdrums.'; nothing goes to IndexedDB in this version. Rule: v2 only ADDS keys and never changes the format of an existing one, so the copy under v1/ and a revert keep working.

Existing keys, read and written exactly as today: spotify.clientId; spotify.tokens (gains two optional fields, scope and authorised_at; a value without them still works); spotify.pending; mic.threshold; mic.centroids; difficulty (the level, global); ear.usual; ear.delays; pick.{spotify track id} (chosen version when following Spotify); fine.{songId}.{md5} (sync tuning per song and version; songId is the Spotify track id, or 'manual.{md5}' for your-own-music sessions, as today).

New keys:
- schema = 2.
- library = { songs: { [songKey]: entry } }, written by js/library.js (this key exists only in uncommitted code and has never shipped, so there is nothing to migrate). songKey = normalised first artist | cleaned title, so a song Spotify played and the same song looked up are one entry. entry = { key, title, artist, album, art, length, charts: up to 6 slim versions, liked (stamp or 0), played, plays, pick (chosen md5), spotify {id, art}, best (overall, kept for the existing test), bests { 'md5|level': run }, runs: the last 12 { at, md5, level, source site|spotify|self, input keys|mic, rate, set, accuracy, grade, score, combo, counts, coverage, label }, parts { 'md5|partId': { tries, accuracy, rate, at, set, by pass|hand, clean {rate, accuracy, at}, last {set, rate} } } }. A slim version is about 400 bytes: md5, name, artist, album, year, genre, charter, length, playLength, tier, levels {level: notes}, nps, drumType, lyrics, twoKick, delay, art (album art md5), pack, modified, real (true, false or unknown). Raw search rows (3 to 70 KB) are never stored. Caps: 300 not-liked songs (oldest opened dropped; liked never), 6 versions and 12 runs per song. Saves are queued outside the animation frame; a failed write is reported with a toast; a 'storage' event from another tab drops the in-memory copy.
- prefs = { view, guideOn, guide (0-1), clickOn, click (0-1), headphones, auto, moveOn, listenFirst, lyrics, lastStart site|spotify|learn|self, hints }.
- last = { key, md5, mode play|learn, part, set, rate, at } for the Continue card.
- own.micDelay = seconds (absent until fitted): the kit-to-microphone delay on the site's own clock, separate from every Spotify tuning.

sessionStorage (per tab): expect { key, md5, title, artist } for the Spotify hand-off; the scroll position of Home's list. In memory only: the last 50 search answers and the parsed charts opened this session.

Backup file (Setup > Export): { app: 'airdrums', schema: 2, exported, library, prefs, last, tunings (fine.* and pick.*), mic {threshold, centroids}, ear {usual, delays}, own {micDelay} }; never spotify.tokens, spotify.clientId or spotify.pending. Import merges with the pure library.merge and refuses a newer schema or a file over 2 MB.

Never in the repo: the Spotify client ID, any token, any real chart, lyric or audio.

Settled with the tally: no new endpoint and no new permission; the zero-call hand-off (Open in Spotify, the poll picks the song up with the chosen version) ships now, liking what Spotify is playing feeds the Liked list, and remote start and the import go to later. The default Play is the site's own clock (against the player), softened by focusing the button used last.
- How much Learn runs itself. QA wanted every step on the player's own click; the teacher, game designer, UX designer and player answered that the learner stands two metres away with both hands full, and the architect that the rules are pure functions Node can test. Tempo steps became automatic by general agreement. I went against the tally twice here. First, automatic drums (F70 scored only 2, with the architect, audio engineer and QA voting cut): I built it in, in the teacher's order (drums come in first at the starting tempo, then only tempo rises) as one stateless rule in the same function as the tempo step, because a hands-free learner on Bass + Snare would otherwise loop at 100% forever with no tick; a fallback to tempo-only is written into the item. Second, the automatic move to the next part is ON by default (QA voted cut, the architect and audio engineer wanted it off by default): it shares one function with the arrow key and the new part opens with an unjudged Listen pass. Thresholds follow the majority: two clean passes at 85% to step up, two under 60% to step down, one clean pass at 100% with all drums to be learned (the teacher wanted two), and marking by hand allowed (the teacher's must, adopted by all).
- The microphone against the site's own sound. The UX designer wanted the guide to play the drums you are not playing during your turn, the teacher and the player a quiet click throughout. The audio engineer, QA and the game designer showed that an untaught mic credits every note written at the moment of any onset, so a guide hi-hat or an on-beat click would score notes for the learner; all three proposers changed their minds. Settled: through speakers the site sounds the counted bar only, then goes silent with a visual beat; Player.mine() is deleted. On the kit-to-mic delay QA wanted no mic judging until a calibration in Setup, the audio engineer a click loopback, the teacher a fit from the learner's own hits. The loopback measures the wrong speaker and was dropped by its own author; I took the teacher's fit (five members) with QA's guard as the display rule: until a fit is clear the pass reads 'not scored yet' and nothing is stored. It is its own late build item, so if it is not green the mic simply lights the pads and Got it carries progress.
- tests/ear_e2e.mjs and line-up by ear. The audio engineer wanted chart time as the reference on every own clock and was ready to rewrite the test; QA and the architect made the test passing unchanged a non-negotiable, and QA found that the test asserts exactly the by-ear line-up on an own clock. The audio engineer in turn showed the architect's 'by ear whenever the site is silent' would line the chart up with the learner's own hits during a silent turn. Settled by who makes the music: three sources. 'site' (the site's drums: chart time, offset 0, by ear off), 'spotify' and 'self' (the user's own record on a silent clock: lyric shift on the notes, by ear on). The demo link and a small 'I'll start the music myself' link use 'self', which also keeps today's typed-song mode alive.
- render.js and how fewer drums are drawn. The UX designer pitched lyrics, score, letters and shapes inside the canvas, the teacher a count row and loop brackets, the game designer note speed, the audio engineer a range argument in five views. QA and the architect held that the five views are the only part checked by eye and nobody can look before shipping; the proposers withdrew one by one. The last open point was a one-line fix so the Drums view stops drawing rings for skipped notes (audio engineer, UX designer) against building the Game from the filtered notes (QA, architect). I chose the rebuild: render.js is not edited at all, every view, the score and the judging agree by construction, and the price (no faint out-of-set notes on the Tab view) is small. Count-in numerals, the part strip, overlays and banners are HTML.
- Storage. The architect argued for splitting the library into an index plus one entry per song now, since the 'library' key has never shipped and this is the one release with no migration; that is true, and I still went with the other six: library.js is the one new module with a passing test, the caps keep the blob small, and re-cutting it is a rewrite inside a refactor nobody can try on the kit. From the architect I kept what costs little: additive keys only, saves outside the frame, failed writes reported, the cache dropped when another tab writes, a schema number for a later split. The IndexedDB cache (cut by three, could by four) waits; loaded charts stay in memory for the session.
- Results: their form, their place in the order, and Spotify runs. QA wanted a #/results route and only a toast after a followed song (the end is guessed from polls and a sheet would cover the next song's first notes); the game designer and the player made a results view after a Spotify run a non-negotiable. Settled: on the site's clock a dialog over the frozen stage (five members); after a followed song a non-modal run card that names the weakest part and opens the same dialog, which gives both sides what they asked for. On order I sided with the teacher, architect, QA and UX designer against the game designer and the player: Learn is built before results, because Learn is one of the five things the owner named and a learner without a results screen still learns; a one-line 'Finished: 87%' stands in until results land. On the synth I took the cheaper fix four members asked for (live voices with per-voice trims and a master gain) but bound it to the audio engineer's numbers, asserted on rendered audio in the rig.

## Risks

- Microphone scoring on the site's own clock has never met a real kit: the hit-delay fit can pick a delay one beat division off on a steady pattern, or never find a clear winner. Mitigation: it runs first on sparse bass and snare notes, shows 'not scored yet' instead of a wrong verdict, can be nudged with [ and ] and reset in Setup, Got it carries progress, and the feature is labelled untried.
- Nobody has heard the synth. Levels and spectra are asserted by numbers, but it may still sound thin or harsh, and Bluetooth output on the PC can add delay the browser under-reports. Mitigation: volumes, Test sound in Setup, guide and click can be switched off.
- Every Spotify change is proven only against stand-in answers shaped like the documentation. The real 429 body, the invalid_grant flow and the slower polling (run ends are read from polls every 5 s) may behave differently. Mitigation: steady() is untouched, no new endpoint or permission, and the v1 copy is one click away.
- The size of the sitting: 16 items with a refactor in the middle. Mitigation: the rig comes first, the play logic is moved and not rewritten, and the stop lines after B09 and B12 allow shipping without the tail (results, mic on the site clock, Spotify hardening, backup). The owner's five named asks are complete at stop line B.
- Automation in Learn (tempo, drums, move on) multiplies the states at a loop wrap. A wrong transition could store progress wrongly or strand the loop. Mitigation: one pure, table-tested step function, one place where it is applied, a written fallback to tempo-only, and Undo on marks made by hand.
- Grouping charts into songs is a guess on free-text titles: two different songs can merge, one song can split, a real title containing 'Live' or 'Cover' gets a label. Mitigation: labels and rank only, nothing but whole-album and 6-fret uploads is hidden, and the version list shows everything.
- The chart site is a third party with a rate limit the page cannot read, weak typo handling and occasional 500s; a long outage leaves only liked and recent songs whose charts still have to be fetched. Mitigation: the paced queue, visible busy and failed states, liked songs keep their versions; a chart-bytes cache is listed for later.
- Headless timing: robot key presses, counted bars and sound-log gaps are asserted to a few milliseconds and may flake on a loaded machine. Mitigation: the robot runs inside the page off the page's own clock, tolerances are stated, and the gate is two green runs in a row.
- Album art has no thumbnails (up to 2.8 MB a picture). Even loaded only for rows on screen, a fast scroll can pull tens of megabytes. Mitigation: 300 ms dwell before a picture is asked for, fixed boxes, tile fallback, none on the play screen.
- localStorage is the only copy of likes, runs and learned parts; clearing the browser loses them and two tabs can still race. Mitigation: export and import, failed writes reported, the storage event, caps on growth.
- Spotify's developer policy says not to build a game on its platform and not to synchronise recordings with visuals. For a private five-user app that only reads what is playing this is a wording and risk matter: the site calls itself a practice and play-along tool, embeds no Spotify audio and shows covers with their source.
- The old #/tab link without Spotify or a demo now lands on Home instead of a play page, and the old 'type a song' form is replaced by lookup plus the 'I'll start the music myself' link: the function is kept, but the owner's habits change. The v1 copy shares storage, so a login made in v2 also works there, but linking again from v1/ needs its own redirect URI.

## Later

- **Pressing Play starts the song on the user's Spotify (search, devices, play endpoints)**: Premium only, three endpoints nobody has run against a live account, and the no-device answer is known only from old forum posts. The zero-call hand-off ships now behind the same button; the remote start can be put in front of it once the owner has tried this release.
- **Spotify Liked Songs: which of my likes have a drum chart**: Needs a new permission and a re-link on the only path proven on the real kit, and spends the unpublished per-developer quota the follow clock lives on. The heart on what Spotify is playing fills the site's Liked list with zero calls. If built later: opt-in button, newest 50 in one call, matched only for rows on screen through the paced queue, cached by track id.
- **Part jumps by Spotify seek while following**: A seek needs Premium, lands about a second off and restarts the judging; a strip that only sometimes works teaches distrust. Part clicks jump on the site's own clock; today's Restart (Spotify) stays.
- **Loop over a run of neighbouring parts**: One part per loop keeps pass closing to one case; runs need a rule for which part earns the progress. Add once single-part loops are proven on the kit.
- **Loop windows of 4 or 2 bars inside a part**: A second loop shape and a sliding window. Parts over 32 s are already cut into 8-bar pieces by parts.js, which covers the worst case with no new control.
- **IndexedDB cache of notes-file bytes per md5**: A chart is 2 or 3 small requests behind a skeleton and loaded charts stay in memory for the session; a second storage layer with its own failures waits for a real need.
- **Library split into an index plus one entry per song**: Sound design, but it re-cuts the one new module that already has a passing test. With 300 unliked songs, 6 versions and 12 runs the single key stays small; the schema number makes a later split a tested one-function migration.
- **Synth voices rendered once to buffers, CC0 drum samples, and the user's own audio file slowed down**: The level problem is solved now by per-voice trims asserted in the rig. Buffers, the Virtuosity CC0 kit and an own-file clock (playbackRate with preservesPitch, lined up by align.js) are the next sound steps and need ears.
- **Drawing changes in render.js: note size, letters and shapes on notes, a count row on Tab, loop shading on the stage (F52, F102, F75, F76)**: The five views are the only part checked by eye and nobody can look before this ships. Everything new on the play screen is HTML around the canvas for now.
- **On 'No drum part': the artist's other songs that have one**: One more search and one more list state; the overlay with 'Find another song' is enough for this version.
- **One cached starter shelf from the chart site's advanced search**: The chart site cannot sort by fame, so a fetched shelf shows songs nobody knows and costs a request, a cache and three states on the first screen. The hand-picked list does the job at zero requests.
- **Full screen button, chrome dimming while playing, start by four hits on the kit (F51, F49, F48)**: Small comforts; hit-to-start turns the microphone into a control that room noise can trigger and cannot be tested without a room.
- **Mic double-hit carry-over for fast notes**: Changes judging on microphone input only, credits notes that were not heard, and cannot be checked without a real kit. Wait for the owner's report on double-bass runs.
- **Installable web app manifest and icons**: Adds nothing to finding, playing or learning a song, and the Spotify login returning into a standalone window cannot be tested.
- **Build stamp, modulepreload list and 'new version, reload' notice**: The service worker already revalidates every file; only the Node import check ships now.
- **Measured frame budget asserted in the rig**: The two rules that prevent stalls are adopted (no storage write or fetch in a frame, text written only on change); a millisecond budget is flaky in headless.
- **Smaller follow-ups: 'next part' label before a change, the strip filling with this run's accuracy, a session summary on leaving Learn, a self-ticking 'Start here' card, kit page 'try it', automatic re-centring of the hit delay (F11, F79, F101, F18)**: Niceties that depend on nothing and that nothing depends on.
- **Re-finding a liked version whose md5 disappeared**: No stored library has the problem yet. When a stored md5 fails to load, the song page already lets the user pick another version.

## Cut

- **Wait-for-me (step) mode**: All seven voted it out, its proposers included: a clock that stops teaches stopping, adds a held state to the one clock everything trusts, and needs the mic to know which drum was hit. Half tempo with bass and snare covers the hard fill.
- **'With the real song': one Spotify replay of a learned part**: Premium-only seek choreography that cannot be tested; 'Play along with Spotify' from the song page covers it.
- **Space and the drum keys changing meaning when the mic is on**: A hidden mode, and the rig, the README and tests/ear_e2e.mjs rely on the keys staying what they are. Enter starts and pauses; Space stays the bass drum.
- **Same-groove detection that turns a family of parts green**: Untuned thresholds would write 'learned' for parts the learner never played, which cannot be told from real progress afterwards.
- **First beats: generated practice patterns**: The owner's rule is the real drum part of a song; the starter list teaches the same beats on songs. The rig gets its fixed chart from a fixture.
- **Join practice with its own ladder and storage**: A second practice type before the first has been tried; a run of two parts can cover it later.
- **'Knowing when to stop' hint**: Timer-driven coaching copy on a guess.
- **Filter and sort chips over the results**: Sorting or filtering 25 loaded rows misleads; the search box is the filter.
- **Recent searches**: The Recent tab already holds what was looked up.
- **Note speed setting**: A multiplier through five drawing routines; in Learn the notes already slow with the tempo.
- **'Watch' a whole song as a demo**: Playing on the site's drums and hitting nothing already is one, and it is honestly reported as 'You watched this one'.
- **Feel pass: combo pulses, flashes, section toasts**: Decoration on top of judging that is itself unverified on the kit.
- **Live hit-error meter under the stage**: Cannot be read from two metres; the results sentence with its fix button says the same.
- **Per-drum accuracy on results**: An untaught mic hit credits every note written at that moment, so the figures would be invented.
- **A microphone that sets its own hit line**: An automatic threshold nobody can try in a room, around the only audio code verified in a real browser. The slider works.
- **Weekly practice trace on Home**: Needs a ledger of practice minutes nobody asked for; the ticks are the trace.
- **Player.mine(): telling the site's guide from the learner's hit by timing**: Its window is exactly where a correct hit lands. Deleted; listening and judging are separated in time instead.
- **Click loopback 'Measure delay' in Setup**: It measures the computer's speaker to the mic, not the kit's own Bluetooth speaker, and would store the wrong number.
- **A #/results route and a shipped ?bot= autoplay hook**: A results route has nothing to show after a reload; a public address that plays itself could write fake bests. Results are a dialog; the robot is injected by the rig.
- **Spotify audio in the page (Web Playback SDK), slowing or looping Spotify, guide drums over Spotify, a YouTube player, Spotify search as the lookup, 30-second previews**: No speed control exists in either Spotify API, the SDK needs DRM and another re-link, YouTube forbids hidden players and overlays, and Spotify search is capped at 10 results on an unpublished quota.
- **Audio or drum stems from chart packages, generated beats for songs with no chart, chart editing or upload**: Copyrighted audio is never fetched, and the owner ruled the real drum part or nothing.
- **Streaks, XP, badges, leaderboards, accounts, cloud sync, playlists, a theme picker, an offline service worker, a framework or build step**: Outside the constraints or the request; the site stays a static practice and play-along tool with everything in the browser.
